Drone seeding initiative in Kenya

James Mwangi May 10, 2026 3 min read Drones, Kenya, Reforestation

In the heart of Kenya's Rift Valley, a groundbreaking reforestation project is taking flight—literally. Using advanced drone technology, a startup called GreenSky is seeding thousands of trees across deforested areas at a fraction of the cost and time of traditional methods.

The initiative, which began in early 2025, has already planted over 500,000 trees across 10,000 acres of land, with ambitious plans to expand to 2 million trees by the end of 2026.

The Challenge of Deforestation

Kenya has lost significant forest cover over the past decades due to agricultural expansion, logging, and urban development. The loss of forests has contributed to soil erosion, reduced water catchment, and loss of biodiversity.

Traditional reforestation methods are labour-intensive, slow, and costly. Hand-planting a single tree can cost several dollars, making large-scale reforestation economically challenging.

How Drone Seeding Works

The process is both innovative and efficient:

  1. Seed preparation: Native tree seeds are specially coated with nutrients and protective materials to enhance germination
  2. Mapping: Drones equipped with advanced sensors create detailed 3D maps of the terrain
  3. Seeding: High-capacity drones fly over targeted areas and release seed pods at precise intervals
  4. Monitoring: Subsequent drone flights monitor germination rates and seedling growth
"We can plant up to 100,000 trees per day with a fleet of drones, compared to 1,000 trees per day with manual planting."
— James Mwangi, GreenSky Operations Director

The Technology Behind It

The drones used in this initiative are specially designed for reforestation:

  • High-capacity seed pods: Each drone can carry up to 10,000 seed pods
  • AI-powered navigation: Drones automatically adjust flight paths based on terrain
  • Precision targeting: Seeds are placed at optimal depths and spacing for maximum germination
  • Real-time monitoring: Live data on seeding progress and success rates

Results So Far

The early results of the drone seeding initiative are encouraging:

  • Cost reduction: Seeding costs reduced by 60% compared to manual planting
  • Speed: 10 times faster than traditional methods
  • Germination rates: 75% germination rate, comparable to manual planting
  • Accessibility: Drones can reach areas inaccessible to ground crews

Community Impact

The initiative has created opportunities for local communities:

  • Jobs: Training local youth as drone operators and maintenance technicians
  • Education: Schools involved in monitoring and learning about reforestation
  • Ecological benefits: Restored ecosystems improve water availability and soil quality
  • Carbon credits: Potential for carbon offset programs to provide additional income

Expansion Plans

Following the success of the Kenyan initiative, GreenSky is planning to expand to other African countries, including Ethiopia, Tanzania, and Rwanda. The goal is to plant 10 million trees across Africa by 2028.

Conclusion

Drone seeding represents a transformative approach to reforestation in Africa. By combining technology with ecological restoration, initiatives like this are not just planting trees—they're building sustainable futures for communities and ecosystems.
